### Why Make an AI Policy

It's impossible to avoid the question of generative AI while blogging about creativity and technology. I mean, it's impossible to avoid the question of generative AI while blogging about *anything*. 

People have very little trust in the authenticity of what they see and read on the internet. As a content creator (much as I may hate that term, that is what I am), the trust you have in me is dependent on my honesty and transparency about if and how I use generative AI tools. To this end, [Damola Morenikeji](https://www.bydamo.la/p/ai-manifesto) encouraged bloggers to create an AI manifesto on their website, so readers would know exactly when they encountered any AI-generated material. 

### My Stance

I am not all or nothing when it comes to AI. The truth is, I'm not concerned about what people do in their own homes on their own computer. But if you put AI-generated content out into the world (or use AI in any way to create content to put out into the world), it carries more power, and danger. Bad actors leverage its power for their own agendas; other individuals, out of ignorance or malice, recklessly bring in their own AI contributions without considering the costs. On the other hand, I believe there is a way to adopt AI responsibly and ethically, both individually and as a society[^1].

The following are some of my biggest concerns about generative AI. These issues inform a lot of my own practices, but this is no way an all-encompassing list of everything I find worrisome. 

#### Misinformation

As social media has become flooded with AI-generated content[^2], it has become a scourge of misinformation[^3]. 

And it's not just about *generative* AI, either. The corporate web is transactional – the product is our data. Our posts, our likes, our follows, our browser history, our searches, etc. Companies who want to sell us something use our personalized data for [targeted advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_advertising) and [personalized marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personalized_marketing). Other entities use this information to try to rewrite the "truth" using [computational propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_propaganda) in[internet manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_manipulation) schemes.

#### Sloppification

AI-generated content is generally of low-to-mediocre quality. And while humans are plenty capable of producing terrible content, the *ceiling* for human-produced work is far above what AI is capable of. 

What AI offers instead is *quantity*. If you can churn out a lot of mediocre content really fast, you can stay at the top of the algorithm and you can fill social feeds. 

#### Crowded-Out Creators

LLMs trained on creative works then generate outputs that mimic the style of those same works, and this devalues the work of creators. Why pay for something if you can prompt a machine to do a bastardized version for free? 

This "crowding out" doesn't just affect artists who sell their work. Independent content creators suffer as well. Today, when you search something in Google, its embedded AI will greet you with an answer before it returns a list of search results. Websites that would have once pulled your attention no longer do[^4].

#### Hallucination and Limitations

LLMs are known to produce [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence)) – to present false information as fact. All too often, people do not verify the information the AI provides. That doesn't just apply to the average person on the internet, but highly educated people performing critical functions[^5].

It is important to recognize that LLMs rely on humans, and the quality and accuracy of their outputs depends on its training data. Generative AI is a recycling machine; it cannot be used to create or learn anything new. As humans we must do that ourselves. 

### How I Use Generative AI

Now, to be clear: all content on this website was written by a human (namely, me, unless otherwise stated). 

That said, I do occasionally use LLMs (Large Language Models) in the process of maintaining this blog and creating new posts. I want to be clear on exactly how I do and don't use these technologies.

| Yes/No | What |

#### Coding

| Yes/No | What |
